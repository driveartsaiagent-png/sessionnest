OneTab lost all your tabs? What to try first

OneTab is a popular way to park hundreds of tabs in one list. Because that list lives only inside the extension on your computer, it can disappear after a browser update, a profile reset or a reinstall. Here is what to check, in this order. (We are not connected with OneTab — this is general advice from people who build tab managers.)

First: don't uninstall or reinstall OneTab. Removing an extension deletes everything it has stored. As long as it stays installed, there is a chance your list is still there.

1. Is OneTab still turned on?

Type chrome://extensions in the address bar (in Edge: edge://extensions) and find OneTab. If it is switched off, switch it back on, then open the OneTab page again (right-click the OneTab icon → Display OneTab).

2. Are you in the same browser profile?

Each Chrome profile has its own OneTab list. If you signed in with a different account or opened a new profile, the old list is in the old profile. Click your profile picture in the top-right corner and open the other one.

3. Did you ever export the list?

If you once used Export / Import URLs and saved the text somewhere (a note, an email, a text file), you can paste it back into OneTab's import box — or into another tab manager.

4. Find the pages in History

Pages you sent to OneTab were opened in your browser first, so they are in History. Press Ctrl+H (Mac: Cmd+Y) and search by site name or by the topic you remember. Chrome keeps about 90 days of history, so older tabs may be gone from there.

5. Backups of your computer

If your computer makes backups (Windows File History, Time Machine on a Mac), an older copy of your browser profile folder may still hold the list. This is an advanced step: close the browser and copy the current profile folder somewhere safe before you restore anything.
