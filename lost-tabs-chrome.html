Chrome lost your tabs after a restart, crash or update? How to get them back

You restarted the computer, Chrome updated itself, or it simply crashed — and the window full of tabs you were working with is gone. In most cases the tabs can still be brought back. Try these steps in order, and don't keep restarting Chrome over and over while you try: each new start can replace the session Chrome remembers.

1. Press Ctrl+Shift+T

Press Ctrl+Shift+T (on a Mac: Cmd+Shift+T). This reopens the last closed tab or window. If Chrome closed with a whole window, one press usually brings the whole window back. Press it again to go further back.

2. Look in "Recently closed"

  1. Click the three-dot menu ⋮ in the top-right corner of Chrome.
  2. Open History. Under Recently closed you'll see lines like "12 tabs" — that's a whole window.
  3. Click it to open all those tabs again.

3. Check your saved tab groups

If you used tab groups, Chrome may have kept them as saved groups. Look on the left side of the bookmarks bar, or in the Chrome menu under Tab groups (the exact place depends on your Chrome version). Click a group to reopen it.

4. Make sure you're in the right profile

If you use more than one Chrome profile (or a guest window opened), the tabs may be in the other profile. Click your profile picture in the top-right corner and open the other profile.

5. Use History to find pages one by one

Press Ctrl+H (Mac: Cmd+Y). Every page you opened is listed by time, so you can find the ones you had open just before the crash and reopen them. This is slow for many tabs, but nothing is lost from History.

6. Stop it from happening next time

Go to Chrome Settings → On startup and choose Continue where you left off. Chrome will then try to reopen your tabs after every restart.

This helps, but it only remembers one session. If Chrome crashes again, or opens a new empty window before you notice, that one session can be overwritten. For tabs you care about, you need a copy that is kept separately.
